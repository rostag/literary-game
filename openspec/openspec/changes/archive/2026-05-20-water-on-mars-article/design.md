## Context

The article will be a standalone markdown document in the repository. No application code, APIs, or services are involved. The primary audience is general readers interested in space science, requiring a balance of technical accuracy and accessibility.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- Synthesize current scientific consensus on water on Mars into a cohesive narrative
- Cover detection methods, geological evidence, and astrobiological implications
- Maintain scientific accuracy while remaining accessible to non-specialists

**Non-Goals:**
- Original research or new scientific analysis
- Detailed coverage of Mars atmospheric chemistry beyond water
- A peer-reviewed publication; this is an explanatory article

## Decisions

- **Structure**: Narrative arc from historical speculation → modern detection methods → evidence summary → habitability implications → open questions
- **Tone**: Authoritative but accessible, avoiding jargon without explanation. Analogies used for complex concepts (e.g., radar sounding compared to medical ultrasound)
- **Citations**: Inline references to primary sources (NASA, ESA, peer-reviewed journals) for key claims, using simple markdown link format
- **Length**: ~2000 words — long enough for depth, short enough for single-sitting reading
- **No diagrams/multimedia**: Pure text markdown. Visual elements can be added in a future iteration

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [Rapid science evolution] → New findings may overtake the article soon after publication. Mitigation: include a "last updated" date and frame open questions as active research areas
- [Accuracy vs accessibility] → Simplifying complex geophysical methods risks misleading readers. Mitigation: cite sources for every quantitative claim and include a "further reading" section
