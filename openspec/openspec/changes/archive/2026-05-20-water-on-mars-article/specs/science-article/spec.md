## ADDED Requirements

### Requirement: Article covers detection methods
The article SHALL describe the primary methods used to detect water on Mars, including radar sounding, spectroscopy, and geomorphological analysis.

#### Scenario: Radar sounding explained
- **WHEN** the article discusses subsurface water detection
- **THEN** it SHALL explain how ground-penetrating radar (MARSIS / SHARAD) identifies liquid water below the Martian south pole

#### Scenario: Spectral evidence covered
- **WHEN** the article discusses atmospheric or surface water
- **THEN** it SHALL describe spectroscopic evidence for hydrated minerals and atmospheric water vapor

#### Scenario: Geomorphological features
- **WHEN** the article discusses historical water
- **THEN** it SHALL reference valley networks, deltas, and lakebeds as evidence of ancient water flow

### Requirement: Article covers geological and astrobiological implications
The article SHALL discuss what water on Mars means for geology and the search for life.

#### Scenario: Habitability discussed
- **WHEN** the article discusses subsurface liquid water
- **THEN** it SHALL address the potential for habitable environments and what that implies for past or present microbial life

#### Scenario: Future exploration mentioned
- **WHEN** the article concludes
- **THEN** it SHALL mention how future missions (e.g., Mars Sample Return, ExoMars) relate to water research

### Requirement: Article is accessible to general readers
The article SHALL be understandable to readers without a planetary science background.

#### Scenario: Jargon explained
- **WHEN** specialized terms (e.g., "spectral signature", "dielectric permittivity", "recurring slope lineae") are used
- **THEN** they SHALL be defined or explained in context

#### Scenario: Analogies used
- **WHEN** a complex detection or measurement method is introduced
- **THEN** an analogy accessible to non-specialists SHALL accompany it

### Requirement: Article cites authoritative sources
The article SHALL cite primary or authoritative secondary sources for scientific claims.

#### Scenario: Claims attributed
- **WHEN** a quantitative or factual claim is made (e.g., "the south polar layered deposit contains a brine layer")
- **THEN** it SHALL include an inline citation to a published source (NASA press release, ESA bulletin, or peer-reviewed paper)
