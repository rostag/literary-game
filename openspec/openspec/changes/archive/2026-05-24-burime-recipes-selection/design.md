## Context

The book "1000+1 Burime for Those Who Have Known Life" contains over a thousand burime recipes — structured poem templates with given rhyme schemes and thematic prompts. A curated selection of the three best recipes requires a systematic evaluation approach and a consistent presentation format.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- Define objective criteria for evaluating burime recipes
- Select the three best recipes based on poetic quality, emotional resonance, and template versatility
- Present each with: rhyme scheme, structural template, example composition, and usage guidance
- Format selections as a standalone markdown document

**Non-Goals:**
- Modifying or rewriting the original recipes
- Creating new burime recipes not from the book
- In-depth literary analysis of each recipe
- Translations of the book's content

## Decisions

- **Selection criteria**: Recipes ranked on (1) poetic craftsmanship — originality and elegance of rhyme/meter, (2) emotional range — depth of themes addressed (love, loss, joy, reflection), (3) template versatility — how easily the recipe can be adapted to different contexts
- **Presentation format**: Each recipe gets a dedicated section with: recipe name, rhyme scheme notation, structural template, a composed example, and practical tips for adaptation
- **Tone**: Warm, appreciative, and accessible — reflecting the book's spirit of making poetry accessible to everyone
- **No quantitative scoring**: Selection is editorial-curatorial, not algorithmic; criteria guide judgment rather than producing a ranked score

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [Subjectivity of selection] → Different readers may prefer different recipes. Mitigation: explain the selection rationale for each chosen recipe
- [Missing context] → Recipes may reference earlier chapters or concepts. Mitigation: include brief contextual notes where needed
